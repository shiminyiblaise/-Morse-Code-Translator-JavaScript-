About Morse Code

Morse code is a method of encoding text characters as sequences of two different signal durations, called dots and dashes. It was developed in the early 1830s and has been used for long-distance communication.

Each letter and numeral is represented by a unique sequence of dots (short signals) and dashes (long signals). For example, the letter "A" is represented as ".-" and "B" as "-...".
